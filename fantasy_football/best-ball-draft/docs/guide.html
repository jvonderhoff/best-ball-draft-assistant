Best Ball Assistant Handbook

Everything the system does, why it's built in two pieces, and the handful of things that will bite you if you forget them.

01 What it is

A draft assistant for DraftKings Best Ball. During a draft it tells you who to take next, using more than the draft-room consensus.

It does three jobs. It gathers projections from several places and averages them. It adjusts them with betting markets, which are real money and therefore a sharper read than any projection site. And it scores who to take now given who's on your roster, where your next pick lands, and your own rankings.

The thing that makes best ball different from normal fantasy: you never set a lineup. Each week your best players are counted automatically. So a player who scores 8, 8, then 35 is worth more than one who scores 15 every week — the 35 wins you a week, the two 8s get dropped. That single fact drives most of the design.

02 The two halves

One system, two programs, in two different places. This is the piece most worth understanding, because nearly every gotcha comes from it.

The draft app

Runs on Render · always on

The website you use during a draft. Holds the player pool and ADP, your rankings board, and both recommendation models.

Always available, including on your phone.

The projections app

Runs on your Mac · only when you start it

Gathers the projections and betting lines, blends them, and sends the finished numbers to the draft app.

Not on the internet. Not on your phone.

Why split at all

DraftKings blocks its betting-line API from data centres. The identical request that works from your house returns "forbidden" from Render. Since betting lines are a real input, the gathering has to happen on a home connection — so it runs on the Mac and pushes the finished numbers up.

The upshot

Your Mac can be asleep during a draft. The draft app keeps working on whatever was last sent to it. What it won't do is update by itself — nothing is automatic except the player pool.

03 Where the numbers come from

Five stages. Only the first is automatic; the rest happen when you run them, which is why the routine in section 6 exists.

01
Automatic · every 6 hours

The player pool and ADP

Who's draftable and roughly when they go. The draft app refreshes this itself.

02
Your Mac · you run it

Three projection sources

Sleeper, ESPN and FFToday each guess a player's season points. Sleeper is re-fetched every time you build; the other two you refresh.

03
Your Mac · home connection required

Betting lines

DraftKings and Underdog season props. These adjust individual pieces of a projection — receiving yards, touchdowns — never the total.

04
Your Mac · you run it

Blend and publish

Everything is combined into one small file and pushed to the draft app, which stores it permanently.

05
Render · live

The draft app scores with it

Until you publish again, this is what your recommendations are based on.

The one that catches everyone

Publishing is manual. Refreshing sources on your Mac changes nothing in production until you publish. The Analysis page looking correct is not evidence that the draft app agrees with it.

A real hole worth knowing

The average only counts sources that have a number. If two sources drop a player to zero because he's injured and one hasn't updated, the stale one becomes the entire answer instead of being outvoted. That's exactly how a receiver on injured reserve carried a 116-point projection into production. Injury status is now read directly from Sleeper, and a player with only one source is flagged on the card.

05 V1 and V2

Two recommendation models run side by side. V1 is the primary column; V2 shows alongside it.

V1

Market-based

Ranks players almost entirely by where the room drafts them, adjusted for value when someone falls past their ADP.

Simple, hard to break, and knows nothing about football.

V2

Projection-based

Uses the ceiling from section 4, plus roster fit, stacking, bye weeks, and how likely a player is to last until your next pick.

Better informed, more moving parts, more ways to be wrong.

They're kept side by side deliberately. Three real V2 errors were caught by eye in its first day purely because a human could see the two columns disagree. V2 is the model under development, not the model in charge.

Recently improved

V2 used to believe players were far more likely to be taken before your next pick than they really are — a permanent "grab him now" nudge. Checked against 33 of your completed drafts, a player it gave a 65% chance of lasting actually lasted 93% of the time. Fixed. You should notice it telling you to wait more often.

06 The routine

Two commands cover almost everything. Both are safe to run whenever.

Before a draft — refresh and publish

cd ~/Development/projects/fantasy_football/projections
export DRAFT_APP_URL=https://best-ball-draft-assistant.onrender.com
tools/refresh-sources.sh

Fetches the betting lines, refreshes FFToday, rebuilds, and stops without sending anything. Read what it prints, then send it:

tools/refresh-sources.sh --publish

Then check what production is actually serving

cd ~/Development/projects/fantasy_football/best-ball-draft
python3 tools/preflight.py

You want PASS — 0 failing. Every check in it exists because that exact thing went wrong once and said nothing at the time.

Looking at the data yourself

cd ~/Development/projects/fantasy_football/projections
export DRAFT_APP_URL=https://best-ball-draft-assistant.onrender.com
.venv/bin/python cli.py analysis-serve

Opens the Analysis page at localhost:8100 — every source, every stat, the Compare view, and the per-player breakdown. Pointed at the Render URL like that, it reads live production data and needs nothing else running.

Always include the export line

That one variable decides both which draft app is read and where a publish is sent. Leave it out and everything silently points at your laptop — and a publish will report success while production never hears about it.

07 Telling if something's wrong

Three places to look, in the order worth looking.

WhereAnswers
tools/preflight.pyEverything at once. Start here.
/setup → Data FreshnessAge of every source, in the browser
The bar on /recommendAppears only when something is genuinely stale

What the freshness panel is telling you

Each source is aged against a clock that suits it, because they don't rot at the same speed. Betting lines go stale in days; the player-ID matching table is fine for a month.

KindStale afterWhy
Projection7 daysFeeds the average directly
Betting lines4 daysMove fast, only refreshable from the Mac
Identity / display30 daysNot a model input at all

The two Sleeper rows show no age on purpose — Sleeper is re-fetched on every build, so a stored age would describe the wrong thing.

Check this before blaming the model

Your rankings board is off by default, per browser. The star in the header is the only indicator: filled blue = on, hollow grey = off. A new browser, a cleared profile, or your phone all start with it off — and an entire analysis was once written against the wrong mode because of it.

08 Traps

Each of these has actually happened, and each was silent — plausible numbers, no error message.

Stale server

The Analysis page does not reload code. Leave it running, change something, and it keeps running the old version indefinitely while looking completely normal. Restart it after any change. Published files now record which version sent them, so this is visible rather than mysterious.

Old numbers on screen

Your browser caches the player list for 10 minutes. If a number looks wrong, open the console and run bustCache('players_bundle'), then reload — twice if it doesn't move, because the first reload serves the old copy while fetching the new one.

Just after a deploy

Deploying resets the player pool to a snapshot stored in the code. The server's storage is wiped each time, so it refills from a committed file until the next automatic refresh. If ADP looks a few days old right after a deploy, that's why — and preflight now says so instead of reporting it as fresh.

Two prop stores

There are two places props can be saved. The Analysis page writes the one the published file reads; an older script writes a different one used only as a backup. If props seem not to matter, check you refreshed the one that counts — the routine in section 6 always does.

09 Glossary

ADP
Average draft position — roughly when a player goes. Unbiased but weak: a quarter of all picks land more than a full round away from it.
Ceiling
Roughly a very good week for a player. The number best ball actually pays for, since your best scores are counted automatically.
Consensus
The plain average of whichever projection sources have a number for that player.
Sources
How many projection sources a player has. One is a warning sign — it means nothing is checking that number.
Availability
Chance the player is active in a given week. Zero if he's on injured reserve, which removes him from consideration entirely.
Props / betting lines
Season over/unders from sportsbooks. Applied piece by piece, never as a total — no book prices receptions, so a prop-only total would badly undervalue receivers.
Survival
The chance a player is still available at your next pick. Now calibrated against your real completed drafts rather than assumed.
The payload
The small file your Mac sends to the draft app. It's what V2 scores with, and it only changes when you publish.
