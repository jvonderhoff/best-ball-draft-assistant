Fantasy Football Map

Four projects, one set of players. Outside data comes in through one place, gets turned into numbers there, and flows out to the app for each kind of game. Click any box to see everything that part does.

One direction

Every outside source is fetched in projections/ and nowhere else. The apps read what it hands them. Nothing flows back except the DK pool and your board.

Ids, not names

Each app keys on its own platform's id: dk_player_id for Best Ball, sleeper_id for Sleeper. They join through one table, players in store.db. If a player can't be matched, he gets reported, not guessed at.

Split by product, not league type

Redraft and Dynasty share an app, because you set a lineup the same way in both; only how a player is valued changes. Best Ball and DFS are both DraftKings but are different products, so they're separate.

Rules worth remembering

  • Weekly numbers come from the market, and only there. The home-made weekly model lost once its test stopped peeking at the season it was predicting.
  • Score exp_td, not anytime_td. One is a count, the other a probability of scoring at all.
  • manual_overrides.json is a to-do list. Every entry is a place automatic matching failed.
  • Publishing is always your call. The nightly job stops at the dry run on purpose.
  • Age comes from generated_at, never the file's timestamp. A copy is stamped “now”.

The recommender

  • V1 (static/recommender.js): built on ADP and roster fit. It's the primary column.
  • V2 (static/recommender-v2.js): built on points, using six fields from Projections: ppg sd sources avail disagreement rec_share. Its key number is ceiling = ppg + 1.28·sd.
  • Will he last to my next pick? That estimate was calibrated on 33 real DraftKings boards, not the simulator.
  • V2 wins at every contest size measured: +43% at an 18-team final, +49% at 45.

Rules worth remembering

  • Pushing to master deploys production. Commit freely; ask before pushing.
  • A 200 doesn't mean your deploy landed. Poll for something only the new build serves.
  • Restart the server after editing a template; it runs without reload.
  • Harness dollar figures are meaningless. Read only V1 vs V2 differences; noise is about ±$2.
  • Check rankings_hydrated on /api/stores/status after a deploy. false means the live board is the seed file.

Redraft and Dynasty are one app. Setting a lineup and working waivers are the same in both; only how a player is valued changes. Sleeper's own settings.type sets horizon: ros here, dynasty there.

The leagues

LeagueSizeLineupNotes
NNCC10 teamsQB 2RB 3WR TE 2FLEX K DEF6pt pass TD, no FAAB
Justice League12 teamsQB 2RB 2WR TE 2FLEX DEF6pt pass TD, FAAB $100, no K

Both full PPR. Your redraft rankings: rankings/2026-redraft-ppr.csv (250 players, with tiers). Never reuse them for best ball or dynasty.

How start/sit decides

  • Availability first. Sleeper's IR/Out/Suspended flags, plus data/unavailable.json for what Sleeper can't say. Every entry there needs an until date.
  • The number is the market's. Where DK hasn't posted a player's prop yet, he falls back to his season rate, and the row says so.
  • Defences are a model built on the opponent's implied total, and counted separately from market prices.
  • The lineup is a maximum-weight matching, not a sort. Flexible slots go to the latest kickoffs so a late scratch can still be swapped.
  • LateRound ranks annotate, never score. They flag a benched player ranked above a starter.
  • It refuses a non-weekly lineup. If no starter took a market number, it prints NOT A WEEKLY LINEUP and stops.

Waivers, and what to bid

  • An add is a pair. Rosters are full, so the question is never "who is the best free agent" but "does he beat the man I would actually cut" — ranked on value over replacement, never raw points.
  • The suggested FAAB bid is fitted on your leagues' real bids (leagues/faab.py, built 14 Sep), in log1p(bid): bids floor at $0 and skew right, and a plain linear fit measured WORSE than guessing the league median ($13.1 against $11.6). The log fit clears it at $11.0 — a real but small edge, and the run prints n and MAE every time so a thin fit never reads as a confident one.
  • A dynasty bid is a third horizon. Rest-of-season value predicts it no better than dynasty market rank (r=0.30 vs 0.29), because the bid buys option value on a career — Hastings' three biggest week-1 bids all went to fringe rookies. So dynasty fits on both, redraft on value alone.
  • Under 15 priced bids it refuses to fit rather than show a number two points produced.
  • It is a proxy, not a backtest — old bids are joined to today's numbers, because no historical stat-line snapshot exists. Read it as "what similar players went for", never as the number to type in.

Dynasty

Two keep-forever Sleeper leagues. The weekly lineup is the same question as redraft. Buying, selling and cutting look at a player's whole career, and the trade market answers those, not a list of yours.

The leagues

LeagueSizeFormatBoard read
Haters Club12 teamsSuperflex, TE +0.5, 10 starters, 15 benchtep
Hastings12 teamsSuperflex, 9 starters, 12 benchvalue

Both full PPR, with 4-point passing TDs. With superflex, what drives quarterback value is how many you have to start, not the scoring.

What's different from redraft

  • Two markets, side by side. Where KTC and FantasyCalc disagree is the signal; a blend would erase it.
  • TE premium is a different board, not a different column. KTC re-solves every player's rank; the output names which board it used.
  • Superflex is verified, not trusted. A 2-QB request whose top 24 isn't QB-heavy gets refused.
  • Waiver cuts look at the whole career. They use the better of the two market ranks, so a young stash doesn't get offered up.
  • Taxi and IR players are never offered as cuts. Dropping one frees no active spot.
  • A FAAB bid is fitted differently here. A dynasty league's bid model uses value over replacement AND dynasty market rank, because the bid buys option value on a career; a redraft league uses value alone.
  • Prices are never points. Start/sit doesn't read them.

Shared with Redraft

Weekly start/sit, waivers, tools/weekly.sh, and every module in leagues/ except dynasty.py. See Redraft →

Joined on

Health
cli.py doctor · 232 tests

How a lineup is built

  • Solved exactly (CBC), not greedily, so no salary is left on the table.
  • --stack pairs a QB with his own pass catcher, only from teams implied at 23+ points.
  • --max-exposure (0.6 default) stops five lineups from being the same bet.
  • --objective mean for 50/50s and double-ups; ceiling for big tournaments.
  • --top-games 2 --min-bringback 1 takes quarterbacks from the highest-total games and adds an opposing player.
  • Defences are projected from the opponent's implied total, so the optimiser no longer just buys the cheapest.
  • Kickers are projected too (showdown only) — without a number they were never selectable at all. The level is the fix; the slope does not pick kickers, and a kicker is never the captain.

what you run by hand · Central time

Your week

Everything you run yourself, on the day to run it. The fantasy week turns over on Tuesday night, when waivers are the question, and ends with Monday night's game — so the days run in that order. What runs by itself is noted on each day; the full list is What runs by itself.

Nothing here acts for you. Every command reads and recommends. Waiver claims, lineups and DFS entries are always set by you, in Sleeper or DraftKings.

Three ways to be wrong without an error

  • The props file is keyed on a name. Look players up through the sleeper_id field. Treat the key as an id and every lookup misses: a complete lineup renders with the market entirely absent.
  • Stat lines are unscored on purpose. Two leagues pay 6 points per passing TD, and a total can't be un-scored.
  • Age comes from generated_at. cp and git clone stamp a copy with “now”; a 31-hour-old file once read as 0.0 h.

Done by hand, on purpose

  • Publishing the payload (refresh-sources.sh --publish or /update-projections). An automated publish once would have shipped a build against a stale cache.
  • The weekly start/sit (sleeper/tools/weekly.sh or /startsit): once, late, before kickoff. /tuesday is the pre-waivers pass.
  • Entering lineups, setting Sleeper lineups, making claims. Nothing here writes to Sleeper or enters a DFS contest.

  • best-ball-draft/tools/com.bba.{standings,autoqueue}.plist
